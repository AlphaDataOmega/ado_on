# On this licence

## It is not open source, and the README should not say it is

The Open Source Initiative's definition (clause 6, "No Discrimination Against Fields
of Endeavor") forbids barring commercial use. A licence that reserves commercial rights
is **source-available**, not open source. Using the right word avoids a fair criticism
and avoids misleading anyone who relies on the label.

## PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 — installed

`LICENSE` is the verbatim text of PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0, preceded by the
`Required Notice:` line the licence's own Notices section specifies. It is not
paraphrased and not modified; a lawyer-drafted instrument that people have already
read and relied on is stronger than clear drafting.

An earlier plain-language draft stood in this position while the machine had no route
to polyformproject.org. It has been replaced.

The corpus carve-out that draft carried now lives in `NOTICE`, because PolyForm
licenses **software** and says nothing about the material in `Δ/`.

## Who holds what

The software copyright is held by **Alpha Data Omega LLC**. The story in `Δ/V·001` is
attributed to **James Sterling Tuttle** as its author.

Whether the story's copyright sits with him personally or has been assigned to the LLC
is a decision for the author, not a drafting matter — it affects who can license it and
what happens to it if the company changes hands. As written, the software is the
company's and the story is the author's. If the intent is that the LLC owns both, say
so in `LICENSE` §4 and in `Δ/corpora.json`; if the intent is that the author keeps the
writing, this is already correct.

## The corpus is not the software

`Δ/V·001` is a story. It is a creative work owned by its author, and it is not covered
by the software licence. This distinction is deliberate: someone may legitimately want
to use the field organism on their own material without acquiring any rights in V's
writing, and someone reading V's story acquires no rights in the code.

Contributors keep their own corpora. `Δ/corpora.json` is where a contributor states
their terms, and a PR that does not state them should not pass the doorway.

## What "training" means here

Section 2 names training, fine-tuning, evaluation, and benchmarking of commercial
systems as commercial use. That is deliberate and worth flagging rather than burying —
it is the use most likely to happen without anyone asking.
