# ZOEVÃ — the derivation spine

Read top to bottom. Each step earns the next; where a step is a *commitment* or *open*, it
says so. Nothing here meets you as a stranger — `3, 6, 9, 100, φ⁷` all arrive in order.
(ADO is short for ZOEVÃ.)

---

## 1 · Begin with closure, not with three

Do not begin `d = 3`. Begin with a requirement: **non-backtracking relational closure** — a
path that returns without reversing itself.

- One state has identity but no relation.
- Two states relate, but any return must retrace the *same* relation — a reflection.
- **Three** is the first cardinality where a path returns by a *different* relation than it
  left on: `A → B → C → A`.

This is the girth-≥-3 fact. **Three is derived as the cardinality of first closure** — the
integer, not yet a spatial dimension.

*Premises made visible:* relations undirected and unique per pair; "non-backtracking"
includes the wraparound; the **minimal** closing cell is taken as the generating unit.

## 2 · Three generates the undirected relational base

Three relata give `C(3,2) = 3` pair-relations: `AB, BC, CA`. These are three base relations,
**not** three Cartesian axes.

## 3 · Orientation is in/out through scale — there is no ±

Each undirected relation is read in **two directions**. Those directions are **not signs**.
The one primitive beneath everything is direction through **scale**: *in* toward the Null
(finer) and *out* away from it (coarser). The trit is **in · null · out**, not
minus/zero/plus. So

    3 relations × (in, out) = 6 directed cross-relations.

In/out is scale's own two directions, so the relations stay undirected at base — nothing
extra to derive, and a truly directed (parallel) relation would collapse closure to two,
which is why it is forbidden. *(This closes what used to be the open ± question.)*

## 4 · Six-plus-three is the nine-place relational field

With the three **self**-relations (the diagonal), the complete field is a 3×3:

    9 = 3 self + 6 cross          (relation-of-relations)
    3 → 6 → 9 : relation → in/out exchange → closed field

## 5 · The frozen tenth

    10 = 1 null + 9 relations

The `1` is not a tenth moving coordinate; it is the **frozen reference** against which the
nine are distinguishable — the still centre, the snake eating its tail (the one winding).
`10 ≡ 9` means *ten is the completed, re-addressed form of nine*, not `10 = 9`. From the next
scale the whole ten becomes a single `1`, cracked bilaterally to expose the next root.

## 6 · Only now, the encoder

One frozen R4 level packs three faces, four states each: `4³ = 64` combinations, held in a
two-digit base-100 pair (64 occupied, 36 spare). `τ` concatenates levels into a prefix
cascade — a coarse address is a strict truncation of a finer one. This is why `000.000.000`
is an **address**, not three ordinary decimals.

## 7 · Base 100 — stated honestly, not derived

ADO has the exact bleed `φ⁷ = 0.034441853…`, the beat `Φ⁷ = 29.034442… = L₇ + φ⁷`, and
`Φ⁷ · φ⁷ = 1`. The often-quoted `Φ⁷(100·φ⁷) = 100` is **trivially true** — it is
`100 · (Φ⁷φ⁷) = 100 · 1`; the 100 sits on both sides, so it does **not** derive base 100
(any `k` gives `Φ⁷(k·φ⁷) = k`). The real per-level alphabet is `4³ = 64`; base-100 is the
two-decimal rendering that holds it. **Open:** a non-trivial derivation of the address base.

## 8 · The irrational ledger

An irrational's finite truncations never exhaust it: `xₙ ≠ x` at every depth while
`xₙ → x`, so the residual `rₙ = x − xₙ ≠ 0` forever. That gives an indefinitely refinable,
ordered ledger with no terminal address — which is why tests used `π, e, √2, φ, √3` rather
than privileging one number. Structure that survives across unrelated irrationals belongs to
the **encoder**, not the irrational.

## 9 · TELE is the finite projection of the trajectory

    irrational trajectory → finite relational projection → address
    xₙ  ─encode→  Aₙ ,   and  A₀, A₁, A₂, …  is the address walk.

So `irrational ≠ address`. The `Z = T` idea is stated carefully as an **equivalence**,
`Zₙ = 𝒜(T≤ₙ)` — the address is reconstructible from the ordered trajectory (and back). **Test
still owed.**

## 10 · The v4 walk

    v4 = 2^(1/4),  v4⁴ = 2
    ½ → ½·2^(1/4) → ½·2^(2/4) → ½·2^(3/4) → 1

The intermediate states are *trajectory* (irrational); the encoder decides each finite
address. Rationals accumulated along the walk go irrational **through the root** (v4, and the
closure's √845 = 13√5), not by summation — a finite sum of rationals stays rational.

## 11 · The half comes from six

Once in/out is established, `6 = 3_in + 3_out` (three relations, each direction). Either
complete direction over the bilateral six is

    3/6 = ½

So the **root is `3/6 = ½`** — reconstructed by counting, no primitive `050`, no half inserted.
`050` is only a base-100 rendering afterward.

## The one paragraph, for the top of any write-up

> ZOEVÃ begins not by assuming three dimensions, but by requiring non-backtracking relational
> closure. Three is the minimum cardinality satisfying it. Its three undirected pair-relations
> become six by reading each one **in and out through scale** — there are no signs, only scale's
> two directions — and with the three self-relations these form the nine-place relational field;
> the frozen null supplies the containing tenth. ADO's recursive encoder records these relations
> level by level, while nonterminating irrational streams provide ordered finite projections
> through that address space. ZOE then asks whether the six-fold in/out bifurcation derives the
> local `3/6 = ½` root, whether `v4 = 2^(1/4)` carries it through a four-step closure, and whether
> a completed nine-plus-null field re-roots under the same relational grammar at the next scale.
